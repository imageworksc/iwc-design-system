# 6 · The H2 cap stays at 44px, over the 42px guideline

**Status** Settled — revisit if the `--one` runs are ever re-measured
**Date** 2026-09-22
**Touches** `--step-3`, `.sec-title`, `.sec-title--one`, `.lead--one`, `.note--one`

## What we decided

`--step-3` keeps its `44px` cap, against the Global UX + Design Rules' desktop
H2 range of 32–42px. Nothing else in the type scale sits outside its range
except [7](7-hero-h1-floor.md).

## Why

Two reasons, and the second is the binding one.

The home page's own h2 is 44px, and the scale is anchored on it.

More importantly, the `--one` variants hold a heading or a lead on a single
line above 1024px, and they do it on **measurements taken at 44px** — the
comment in the system reads *"the heading is 791px at 44px and the paragraph
837px at 18px, against 944px of room at this breakpoint."* Dropping to 42px
does not break them, but it invalidates the numbers that prove they fit, and
those numbers are the only thing standing between that rule and a heading that
silently overflows on some page nobody re-checked.

It is also a small miss. Every width from 538px to the design width is inside
32–42. Only the cap is over, and by 2px.

## What we gave up

Literal compliance with a guideline that says of itself: *"These are
guidelines, not rigid specifications. The visual hierarchy matters more than
hitting an exact font size."*

## What breaks if this changes

The `--one` variants, until someone re-measures all of them at 42px and updates
the comments with the new numbers. That is the work this record exists to name.
